🚦 Bengaluru Traffic & Road Safety Analysis
 
 Project Overview

This project presents a comprehensive Data Analysis and Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) of road safety and traffic enforcement data from Bengaluru. The objective is to understand road crash patterns, fatalities, injuries, traffic violations, enforcement activities, fine collection, automated enforcement, and drink-driving cases using historical data.

The project combines multiple datasets covering city-level road crashes, police-station-level crashes, traffic violations, enforcement and fines, and monthly drink-driving cases. The analysis transforms raw traffic data into meaningful visualizations, Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), trends, and actionable insights.

The main goal of this project is to demonstrate how data analytics can be used to identify road-safety challenges, high-risk locations, major traffic violations, and changes in traffic enforcement patterns.

Project Objectives
Analyze the overall road crash trends in Bengaluru.
Study yearly changes in fatal and non-fatal crashes.
Analyze the number of people killed and injured in road crashes.
Identify stations with higher numbers of road crashes.
Analyze crash patterns across different zones.
Identify the most common traffic violations.
Analyze traffic violations by category.
Study over-speeding violation trends.
Analyze yearly traffic enforcement cases.
Analyze total traffic fines collected over time.
Study the growth of automated traffic enforcement.
Analyze monthly drink-driving cases.
Calculate important road-safety and enforcement KPIs.
Study relationships between crash and enforcement indicators using correlation analysis.
Generate data-driven recommendations for improving road safety.

📊 Key Insights

The analysis highlights several important patterns in Bengaluru's road-safety data:

Road crashes show significant variation across different years.
Fatal and non-fatal crashes have different patterns and levels of severity.
Some police stations contribute a much larger share of total crashes and fatalities.
Traffic violations are concentrated in a few major offence categories.
Helmet-related violations form a major part of traffic enforcement activity.
Over-speeding cases vary across years and can be used to understand enforcement and compliance patterns.
Traffic fine collection changes over time along with enforcement activity.
Automated enforcement represents an important part of modern traffic management.
Drink-driving cases show monthly variation in the available data.
Road-safety risk is not evenly distributed across Bengaluru, highlighting the importance of location-specific interventions.

🏁 Conclusion

This analysis provides a data-driven overview of Bengaluru's road-safety and traffic-enforcement situation. The findings show that road crashes and fatalities vary over time and that road-safety risk is not evenly distributed across locations.

Station-level analysis helps identify areas with higher crash and fatality burdens, while traffic-violation analysis highlights the importance of safety-compliance enforcement. The analysis of enforcement cases, fines, automation, and drink-driving provides additional insight into Bengaluru's broader traffic-management environment.

Overall, the project demonstrates how Python-based data analytics can transform raw traffic data into actionable insights and support better decision-making for road safety, targeted enforcement, and traffic management.
